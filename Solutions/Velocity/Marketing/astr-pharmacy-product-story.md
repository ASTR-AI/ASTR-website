# ASTR AI pharmacy software — website story and content draft

Prepared 25 October 2026. This is a proposed marketing narrative, not a published page or an approved implementation design. The user has confirmed the product name Velocity and the destination website https://www.astrai.in/. Use “Velocity by ASTR AI” as the product identity. Code inspection establishes implementation evidence, not production acceptance or measured customer outcomes.

## Purpose

Help hospital leaders, pharmacists, store teams, procurement staff, and accountants understand what the software does and why it belongs in their daily work. Standalone pharmacy owners should find their needs quickly without having to read the hospital workflow.

Primary action: Book a demo. Secondary action: Explore the workflow. Do not offer a video or interactive demo until one exists.

## Recommended direction

Tell the story of a medicine moving through the business: purchase, receipt, storage, pharmacy supply, sale or ward use, and accounting. Each chapter answers a practical question and shows an actual product screen with synthetic demonstration data.

The cinematic opening should give way to a clear, searchable feature reference. A visitor can understand the product in a short scan, then investigate every relevant module.

Alternatives for discussion:

- Workflow story, recommended: one connected journey makes the system understandable and gives animation a purpose. Requires a carefully prepared set of real product captures.
- Audience-led page: separate hospital and pharmacy paths immediately. Makes relevance obvious but repeats shared features and divides the story early.
- Feature-led page: show the complete module catalogue first. Easy to scan for known requirements, but makes the product harder to understand for a first-time buyer.

## Chapter 1 — The everyday problem

### A busy pharmacy needs clear answers.

Is this medicine available? Is it in the store or at the counter? Has the ward received its supply? Which supplier still needs to be paid?

When these answers are spread across registers and separate files, even a simple task takes extra work.

### Bring the work into one view.

Connect your stock, purchases, transfers, and sales so your team can follow the work from start to finish.

Visual: introduce these questions one at a time, then reveal the relevant areas of the software. Present this as an illustrative situation, not a measured description of every customer's current process.

## Chapter 2 — Inventory

### Know what you have. And where it is.

Check medicine stock across your warehouse, pharmacy, and hospital locations. Follow batches and expiry dates, and see quantities in the packs and units your team uses.

Supporting details:

- Medicine catalogue and categories.
- Stock by location, medicine, and batch.
- Expiry and low-stock information.
- Pack and loose-unit quantity display.
- Permission-controlled stock adjustments with a recorded history.

Visual: hold one inventory screen in view while highlighting location, batch, expiry, and quantity in sequence. Let each label stay readable before the next appears.

## Chapter 3 — Reordering

### Plan your next order with a clearer picture.

Review reorder suggestions based on available stock and past use. See which medicines need attention and use the recommendations to prepare purchase orders.

Supporting copy: The system considers recent consumption, seasonal history where available, stock nearing expiry, and supplier lead time. Your team reviews the recommendation before ordering.

Visual: show the real reorder view, then a suggested quantity and the purchase-order preparation step. Any displayed quantities and graphs must be clearly identified as demo data.

Editorial rule: call this “demand forecasting” or “reorder recommendations.” Do not imply an autonomous AI buyer or guarantee that shortages will never occur.

## Chapter 4 — Purchasing

### From order to received stock. Keep every step clear.

Create purchase orders, review approvals, and record the medicines you receive. Keep supplier bills and payment records connected to your purchasing work.

Supporting details:

- Supplier records and contact details.
- Purchase-order creation, approval, rejection, and cancellation.
- Goods receipt with batch and expiry details.
- Direct purchase receipt and bill entry.
- Controlled correction of received bills.
- Supplier payment recording and payment history.

Visual: an order moves through its actual supported states, ending in the received-stock screen. Do not animate an approval or receipt as automatic when a user action is required.

## Chapter 5 — Hospital supply

### Keep the pharmacy and the hospital store connected.

Follow supply requests through picking, dispatch, and receipt. See what is being prepared, what has been sent, and what has arrived.

Supporting copy: Move stock between locations with approval workflows. Track warehouse supply runs and their return process when a delivery is rejected.

Visual: use the real request, picking, dispatched, and accepted states as a short scroll sequence. Highlight the next responsible team at each step.

## Chapter 6 — The counter and the ward

### Ready for the counter. Connected to the ward.

For pharmacy teams: Create sales, record payments, review transaction history, and download bills. Track credit settlements and the daily sales summary.

For hospital teams: Record medicines issued to wards, track consumption, and manage returns to the pharmacy. Supported ward workflows include scan-based issue and consumption.

Visual: show two clearly labelled product views, “Pharmacy counter” and “Hospital ward.” Hospital-specific details must remain understandable as optional modules to standalone pharmacy buyers.

## Chapter 7 — Accounts

### Make the next accounting step easier.

Keep supplier payment records organised and prepare pharmacy data for your accounts team. Export Tally XML and GST audit spreadsheets from the accounting workspace.

Supporting details: Organization and GST settings, ledger account mapping, export preview, and export history.

Visual: show the real export preview and available download types. Describe this as an export workflow; do not imply continuous two-way Tally synchronisation or automatic tax filing.

## Chapter 8 — Management and accountability

### See what needs your attention.

Review inventory, purchasing, supplier, hospital, and financial reports. Find low-stock items, stock nearing expiry, and medicines that move slowly.

### Give each person the access their work needs.

Use role-based permissions and approval workflows to organise responsibilities. Review recorded inventory activity when you need to understand a change.

Visual: one report at a time, with its date range and data freshness visible. Some reports use snapshots; do not describe all charts as continuously live.

## FAQ content

**Who is it for?** Hospital pharmacies and independent pharmacies that need to manage medicine stock, purchasing, and counter operations. Hospitals can also use the ward and internal supply workflows included in their setup.

**Can we see stock across locations?** The software includes warehouse, pharmacy, and location-based stock views, with transfer and supply workflows.

**Does it track batches and expiry dates?** Batch and expiry information is part of inventory and receiving, with reporting to help identify expiry risk.

**Can it help with reordering?** It provides recommendations using stock and consumption history. Your team reviews the suggestions and prepares the order.

**Can our accounts team use the data?** The accounting workspace supports Tally XML and GST audit spreadsheet exports, with ledger mapping and export previews.

**Which modules will we receive?** Module availability depends on the configured setup. Confirm the offered package during the demo.

Pricing, migration, training, support hours, integrations, hosting, backup commitments, and rollout time require the business owner's confirmed terms before public answers are written.

## Scroll and motion direction

Use the presentation principles suggested by Apple's product pages: an immediate promise, a large product visual, short benefit-led chapters, and deeper detail after the highlights. The motion below is a proposal, not a claim about Apple's exact implementation.

1. Hero: one restrained screen reveal; headline and demo action are visible immediately.
2. Overview: a gentle zoom from the dashboard into stock information.
3. Inventory: a short sticky sequence highlights location, batch, expiry, and quantity.
4. Supply: an order progresses through the real workflow states as the visitor scrolls.
5. Accounts: transition from a payment record to the export preview.
6. Ending: return to the whole dashboard and a clear demo invitation.

Use normal document scrolling. Avoid scroll hijacking, repeated full-screen pinned sections, tiny dashboard text, and essential copy that stays hidden until an animation finishes. On mobile, use stacked screens and simple reveals. Reduced-motion users receive the complete static story. Lazy-load lower-page media, reserve image dimensions, and test on ordinary phones. Do not promise performance before measuring the built page.

The marketing page must follow ASTR AI's existing website identity once supplied. Product screenshots should preserve the software's canonical design system. This document defines a narrative, not a competing UI design system.

## Reference review

Healthray reference: https://healthray.com/pharmacy-management-system/

Useful content patterns observed: explicit category positioning, hospital and retail audience descriptions, grouped features, a hospital-versus-retail explanation, FAQs, and repeated demo invitations. These support buyer comprehension and requirement checking.

At the captured desktop hero, the lead form takes roughly half the screen before the product itself is shown. The introductory section contains long paragraphs alongside an illustrative interface. For ASTR AI's requested storytelling direction, put an actual product view early and shorten the first explanations. This is a design recommendation, not evidence of Healthray's conversion performance.

The hero form has six visible business/contact fields plus a privacy checkbox. Its visual prominence is useful for visitors already ready to enquire, but it gives first-time visitors more to process. Proposed ASTR treatment: a clear demo button in the hero and a short form after the story.
